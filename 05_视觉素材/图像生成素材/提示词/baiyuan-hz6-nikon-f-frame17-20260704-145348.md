# BAS-HZ6-R06-F17 prompt

Generate a single fictional archival photograph from the White Abyss project.

Image identity:
- Photo ID: BAS-HZ6-R06-F17
- Date: November 18, 1961
- Photographer: Thomas E. Mallory
- Agency: Bureau of Antarctic Survey
- Event: HZ-6 Sample Line Field Record
- Location: HZ-6 Sample Line, twilight conifer layer beneath Antarctic ice
- Camera: Nikon F 35mm SLR
- Lens: Nikkor-S 50mm f/1.4
- Film: Kodak Tri-X Pan, ASA 400, pushed and poorly developed
- Frame type: emergency handheld flash photograph, 35mm horizontal frame, 3:2 aspect ratio

Scene:
A failed black-and-white accident photograph taken during the HZ-6 field incident. The camera is low and tilted, as if the photographer has just stumbled or half-kneeled in wet ground. In the near foreground are pale frost, black conifer roots, wet mineral mud, and a partial boot edge blurred by motion. Mid-frame, Lt. Howard P. Rusk is turning sharply toward the tree line, only partly visible in a heavy polar field parka. His face is not clear. Behind him, between primitive black conifer root plates, there is the low, animal-like silhouette of HZ6-CO-01. It must not be clearly visible: only a long back line, high shoulders, one overlong forelimb, and a dark wet mass partly swallowed by flash bloom and motion blur. The front of the animal is hidden by overexposure, tree roots, and camera shake.

Lighting and photographic failure:
Use a single 1961 flashbulb burst in a dark under-ice forest. The flash overexposes frost and wet roots in the center while the edges fall into crushed black. The shutter is slow, around 1/15 second, with strong hand shake and subject motion blur. The flash did not freeze the whole scene. The image should feel like a damaged forensic print made from a difficult negative, not a cinematic still.

Archive look:
Make the final image look like a damaged, low-resolution archival scan of a mid-20th-century black-and-white gelatin silver print, not a restored high-definition photograph. The subject should be only partially legible. Reduce apparent sharpness across the whole frame. Use soft focus, slight lens misalignment, film grain clumping, scanning blur, chemical fog, silvering, dust, scratches, water stains, uneven development, crushed shadows, blown frost highlights, pressure marks near the film edge, and low micro-contrast.

Strict constraints:
- No color.
- No modern digital camera look.
- No cinematic polish.
- No clean monster design.
- No clear face, eyes, teeth, claws, or full creature body.
- No gore.
- No readable text, labels, logos, watermarks, captions, or UI.
- No modern military gear, LED lights, night vision, computers, GPS, or smartphones.
- Do not make the animal humanoid. It can have one slightly hand-like forelimb impression, but it should primarily read as a primitive large undergrowth animal.
- Do not make the composition beautiful or heroic. It should look accidental, frightening because it is incomplete.
