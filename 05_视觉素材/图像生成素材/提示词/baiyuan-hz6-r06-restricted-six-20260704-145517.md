# HZ-6/R06 restricted six prompt pack

Shared generation rules for all six images:

Generate one fictional archival photograph from the White Abyss project. The image must look like a damaged, low-resolution scan of a 1961 black-and-white 35mm gelatin silver print made from a Nikon F negative, not a modern restored image and not a cinematic still. Use Kodak Tri-X Pan ASA 400 pushed film, harsh flashbulb lighting when specified, soft focus, hand shake, motion blur, crushed blacks, blown frost highlights, chemical fog, silvering, dust, scratches, water stains, uneven development, pressure marks near the film edge, low micro-contrast, and scanning blur. No color, no modern digital clarity, no readable text, no labels, no logos, no watermark, no modern equipment, no gore, no clear monster design. HZ6-CO-01 must never appear as a clear creature: only partial silhouette, shoulder line, forelimb impression, wet dark mass, or ambiguous reflection.

## Frame BAS-HZ6-R06-F17

Photo ID: BAS-HZ6-R06-F17. Date: November 18, 1961. Photographer: Thomas E. Mallory. Agency: Bureau of Antarctic Survey. Camera: Nikon F 35mm SLR, Nikkor-S 50mm f/1.4, Kodak Tri-X Pan ASA 400. Emergency handheld flash photograph, horizontal 3:2 frame.

A failed accident photo in the twilight conifer layer beneath Antarctic ice. The camera is low and tilted, as if Mallory has stumbled or dropped to one knee. Pale frost and black primitive conifer roots fill the lower frame. Lt. Howard P. Rusk turns sharply toward the tree line, only partly visible in a heavy polar field parka, his face lost to blur. Behind him, between root plates, a low animal-like silhouette of HZ6-CO-01 is barely present: long back line, high shoulder, one overlong forelimb impression. The front of the animal is swallowed by flash bloom, root shadows, and camera shake.
